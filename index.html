Slide 3 · State Landscapes

Four landscape regions

Like the New York landscape map on page 10 of the ESRT, Texas divides into regions with similar landforms, rock and elevation. The land climbs from sea level on the coast to over 8,000 feet in the west.

Gulf Coastal Plains

The largest region covers the eastern and southern half of the state, from the Piney Woods of East Texas to the beaches and barrier islands of the coast. The land is low and fairly flat, built from layers of sediment that rivers carried toward the Gulf. Houston, Dallas and San Antonio all sit here.

North Central Plains

West of the coastal plains, rolling prairies and low hills rise to about 900–2,000 feet. Grasslands and cross-timber forests grow on limestone and sandstone, and the land is used for ranching and farming. Fort Worth is on its eastern edge.

Great Plains

A high, flat tableland that includes the Panhandle's Llano Estacado and the Edwards Plateau (Hill Country). The Caprock Escarpment marks its eastern edge, and the plains rise above 4,000 feet near New Mexico. Rivers have carved deep canyons such as Palo Duro into the plateau.

Mountains and Basins

West of the Pecos River, rugged mountain ranges alternate with dry desert basins of the Chihuahuan Desert. This is the only truly mountainous part of Texas, home to Guadalupe Peak and Big Bend National Park. It gets the least rain in the state, often under 12 inches a year.

Slide 4 · Sun & Stars: Apparent Motion and Position

The sky over Texas's 3 largest cities

Slide 5 · Hydrosphere and Geosphere/Lithosphere

Water and rock of Texas

Hydrosphere Water features

River · ≈1,900 mi long

Rio Grande

The Rio Grande forms about 1,250 miles of the border between Texas and Mexico. Over millions of years it carved deep canyons like Santa Elena Canyon in Big Bend, with walls 1,500 feet high. Its water irrigates farms in the Rio Grande Valley, and so much is used that parts of the river sometimes run nearly dry.

Aquifer · cavern-filled limestone

Edwards Aquifer

This underground layer of porous, cave-riddled limestone stretches about 180 miles in an arc through Central Texas. It is the main source of drinking water for more than 2 million people, including the city of San Antonio. Where its water reaches the surface it feeds Comal Springs and San Marcos Springs, the largest springs in Texas.

Lake · ≈25,400 acres

Caddo Lake

Caddo Lake on the Texas–Louisiana border is often called the only large natural lake in Texas; nearly all the others are reservoirs behind dams. It formed when a huge logjam called the Great Raft blocked the Red River in the 1800s. Its maze of bayous holds one of the largest flooded cypress forests in the world.

Geosphere / Lithosphere Landforms & rock

Canyon · ≈120 mi long, 800 ft deep

Palo Duro Canyon

Palo Duro Canyon in the Panhandle is the second-largest canyon in the United States. The Prairie Dog Town Fork of the Red River eroded it into the Caprock edge of the High Plains. Its red, orange and white rock layers record about 250 million years of Earth's history.

Granite dome · rises 425 ft

Enchanted Rock

Enchanted Rock is a huge dome of pink granite in the Hill Country, formed from magma that cooled underground over a billion years ago. Erosion stripped away the softer rock above it, and its surface now peels off in sheets through exfoliation. At night the rock creaks as it cools and contracts, which gave it its name.

Mountains · peak 8,751 ft

Guadalupe Mountains

The Guadalupe Mountains are made of the Capitan Reef, a fossil reef that grew in a shallow sea about 260 million years ago. Uplift later raised the limestone reef thousands of feet into the air. The range holds Guadalupe Peak, the highest point in Texas, and the sheer cliff of El Capitan.

Slide 6 · Fun Facts

Only in Texas

1836–1845

Texas was its own country, the Republic of Texas, for nearly 10 years before joining the United States.

1.5 million

Mexican free-tailed bats live under Austin's Congress Avenue Bridge in summer, the largest urban bat colony in North America.

825,000 acres

The King Ranch in South Texas is bigger than the entire state of Rhode Island.

302 ft

The Texas Capitol in Austin is taller than the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C. It is built of pink “sunset red” granite quarried near Marble Falls.

254

Texas has 254 counties, more than any other state.

#1

Texas produces more wind power than any other state, mostly from turbines on the windy plains of West Texas.

1885

Dr Pepper was invented at a drugstore in Waco, Texas.

6 flags

Six nations have flown their flags over Texas: Spain, France, Mexico, the Republic of Texas, the Confederacy and the United States.

El Paso

El Paso is closer to San Diego, California than it is to Orange, Texas, on the state's eastern border.
